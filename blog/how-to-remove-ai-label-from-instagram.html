Why Does Instagram Show an "AI Info" Label?

Meta doesn't guess which images are AI. It reads what the files say about themselves.

In February 2024, Meta announced that it would label AI images on Facebook, Instagram and Threads by detecting industry-standard markers: the C2PA and IPTC metadata that tools from Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Adobe, Midjourney and Shutterstock write into their images.

The first version of the label said "Made with AI". Photographers objected almost immediately, because real photos with small edits were getting it. In July 2024 Meta renamed it "AI info". Then in September 2024 it moved the label into the post's menu for images that were only edited with AI, and kept it visible under the username for images detected as fully AI-generated.

So the label you see depends on what your file claims, and Meta has changed the wording more than once. In my own test (below), it appeared under the account name as "AI content".

Instagram reads two kinds of metadata.

C2PA Content Credentials

C2PA (the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is an open standard backed by Adobe, Microsoft, Google, Sony, Leica and others. A C2PA manifest is a signed record stored inside the file. It says which app made or edited the image and what it did, including whether AI was involved. Adobe Firefly, Photoshop's Generative Fill and ChatGPT's image generator all write one. Because the record is signed, nobody can quietly change it; you can only keep it or remove it. Learn more about C2PA Content Credentials.

IPTC and XMP fields

IPTC and XMP are older, plainer metadata formats that sit alongside EXIF. A few of their fields matter here:

  • DigitalSourceType says how the image was made. The value trainedAlgorithmicMedia means "made by AI", and compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia means a real image combined with AI elements.
  • AISystemUsed (IPTC "AI System Used") names the generator, such as Adobe Firefly, DALL-E 3, Midjourney or Stable Diffusion.
  • Software and History record the app that saved the file and, sometimes, a log of edit steps such as Generative Fill.

Phones do this too. AI edits made with tools like Google's Magic Eraser or Samsung's Galaxy AI can leave the same kind of marker.

The important part: the label reacts to what the file says, not to what the photo looks like. A tag that says "AI touched this" is enough, even if 99% of the picture came straight off your camera.

What If the Label Still Appears?

Metadata is the most common trigger, but it isn't the only one. Meta itself said in its 2024 announcement that it can't detect all AI content from markers and that people can strip them, so it looks for other signals as well. If a cleaned image is still labelled, it's usually one of these. (For other ways to strip metadata, such as ExifTool or screenshots, see our comparison of four methods to remove AI metadata.)

1. An invisible watermark in the pixels

Some generators hide a signal in the image itself. Google's SynthID is the best-known example: it shifts pixel values in a pattern people can't see but software can detect. It isn't metadata, so no metadata tool can remove it without changing the picture.

2. Detection from the image itself

Platforms can also run classifiers that look at the picture rather than the file, checking for patterns that generative models tend to leave behind. Cleaning metadata has no effect on those.

3. The label is on an old post

Cleaning a file only helps the next upload. Posts that are already live keep their label. To get rid of it, you'd have to delete the post and upload the cleaned copy as a new one.

When You Shouldn't Remove the AI Label

Removing a false label from a real photo is fair. Hiding the fact that an image was made by AI is something else.

  • Platform rules: Meta requires people to disclose realistic AI-generated video and audio and says it may apply penalties if they don't. For images, it gives you a way to add the label yourself.
  • Your audience: if an image is entirely or mostly generated, leaving the label up is the honest choice, and people trust creators who are open about it.
  • A simple rule: clean the metadata when AI only helped you edit a real photo. Keep the label when AI made the picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Why did my real photo get flagged with an AI label on Instagram?

Because the file said AI was involved. Photoshop, Lightroom and many phone gallery apps write a C2PA or IPTC tag when you use an AI feature such as generative fill, object removal or AI background removal. Instagram reads that tag when you upload and labels the post, even when almost all of the photo is a real camera capture.
