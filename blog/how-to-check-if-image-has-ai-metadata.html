What Is AI Metadata and Where Does It Live?

An image file holds two things: the picture (the pixels you see) and metadata, text stored inside the file that you don't see. Think of metadata as a receipt stapled to the back of a print. Your camera writes the first lines; every app that edits or exports the file can add more.

When AI is involved, it usually shows up in one of four places:

  1. C2PA Content Credentials: a signed record of how the file was made and edited, backed by Adobe, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI and others. ChatGPT/DALL-E, Adobe Firefly, Photoshop's generative tools and Samsung's Galaxy AI (S25 and later) write one. More on C2PA.
  2. IPTC and XMP fields: standard photo-industry fields. DigitalSourceType says how the image was made, and AISystemUsed can name the generator. Google Photos uses IPTC to mark images edited with Magic Editor and Magic Eraser.
  3. Prompt text: Midjourney usually stores your full prompt, parameters and Job ID in the image's Description field. Stable Diffusion tools such as Automatic1111 write the prompt, seed, sampler and model hash into a PNG text chunk called parameters; ComfyUI saves its whole workflow.
  4. Software and history fields: the Software tag and edit history can name the app or feature used, such as Generative Fill.

Method 2: Check C2PA with Content Credentials Verify

If you specifically want to read a C2PA record, the Content Authenticity Initiative runs a free checker at contentcredentials.org/verify. Upload the image and it shows who signed the credentials, which app made or edited the file, and whether AI was used.

It's excellent for C2PA, but it only reads C2PA. It won't show IPTC fields, Midjourney prompts or Stable Diffusion parameters, so "no Content Credentials" there doesn't mean "no AI metadata".

Method 3: Use the Built-In Tools on Windows and Mac

Windows (File Properties)

  1. Right-click the image and choose Properties.
  2. Open the Details tab.
  3. Scroll through the Origin, Camera and Advanced photo sections. The Program name field shows the software that saved the file.

Windows shows basic EXIF and a few IPTC fields only. It can't read C2PA or PNG text chunks.

Mac (Preview)

  1. Open the image in Preview.
  2. Press Command + I, or choose Tools > Show Inspector.
  3. Click the (i) tab, then the EXIF or IPTC sub-tab if it appears.

Like Windows, Preview shows the basics but not C2PA.

For advanced users: ExifTool

The free command-line tool ExifTool reads almost everything. Run exiftool -a -G1 photo.jpg to list every field with the group it belongs to.

Method 4: Adobe Photoshop

  1. Open the image and go to File > File Info (Alt + Shift + Ctrl + I on Windows, Option + Shift + Command + I on Mac).
  2. Check the IPTC, IPTC Extension and Raw Data sections.
  3. In Raw Data, search for DigitalSourceType, AISystemUsed or generative.

Recent versions of Photoshop also have a Content Credentials panel (under the Window menu) that shows the C2PA record for images that carry one.

Method 5: Check on iPhone and Android

iPhone (Photos app)

  1. Open the photo and swipe up, or tap the (i) button.
  2. Read the details panel. It shows the camera or the app the image was saved from.
  3. Photos edited with Apple's Clean Up tool can show a note that they were modified with Clean Up.

The iPhone doesn't show C2PA or IPTC AI fields, so use Method 1 in Safari for a full check.

Android: Google Photos

Open the photo and swipe up to see Details. Since October 2024, Google Photos shows an "AI info" section reading "Edited with Google AI" for photos changed with Magic Editor, Magic Eraser or Zoom Enhance.

Android: Samsung Gallery

Images changed with Galaxy AI's Generative Edit carry a visible "AI-generated content" watermark in the corner of the picture. On the Galaxy S25 and phones updated to One UI 7, Samsung Gallery can also show the image's Content Credentials.

What Metadata Can and Can't Tell You

It can show

  • every tag stored in the file: EXIF, IPTC, XMP, C2PA and PNG text;
  • which app or AI feature made or edited the image, if the app recorded it;
  • prompts and generation settings, if the generator saved them;
  • private details like GPS location and camera serial number.

It can't show

  • Invisible watermarks: Google's SynthID is hidden in the pixels themselves, not the metadata, so metadata tools won't see it.
  • What an AI detector will say: detectors judge the picture itself, not the file's tags.
  • Proof an image is real: screenshots, re-saves and most social apps strip metadata. An AI image with its metadata removed looks "clean" to every method above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Can I check AI metadata on a smartphone?

Yes. TryMetaClear works in Safari, Chrome or Samsung Internet without installing an app. Google Photos also shows an "AI info" section for photos edited with Google AI, and Samsung Gallery on the Galaxy S25 and later can show Content Credentials.

Q2: Does checking an image's metadata change the file?

No. Viewing metadata is read-only. Your original file on your device isn't modified, compressed or re-saved. When you scan with TryMetaClear, a copy is sent to our server, read, and deleted straight afterwards.

Q3: Why does my camera photo have AI metadata if I didn't generate it?

Because an editing feature added it. Generative tools such as Photoshop Generative Fill, Lightroom Generative Remove, Samsung Generative Edit and Google Magic Editor write C2PA or IPTC tags into the file, even when they only changed a small part of a real photo.

Q4: What is the difference between EXIF data and C2PA metadata?

EXIF records camera details: device model, lens, shutter speed, ISO, date and often GPS location. C2PA is a signed record of how the image was made and edited, including whether AI was involved and which app did it.

Q5: If an image has no AI metadata, does that mean it isn't AI?

No. Metadata is easy to remove, and screenshots, re-saves and many social apps strip it. No AI metadata only means the file doesn't say it was made with AI. It is not proof that it wasn't.
